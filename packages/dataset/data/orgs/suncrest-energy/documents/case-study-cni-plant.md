# Case Study: 4 MW Rooftop + BESS for a Textile Plant

*Suncrest Energy Systems — Customer story*

> Fictional customer and figures, created for demonstration purposes.

## At a glance

- **Customer:** A vertically integrated textile manufacturer (spinning, weaving, processing) near Ahmedabad, Gujarat
- **Scope:** 4 MWp rooftop solar across six sheds, 2 MWh VaultBESS, SunSight Optimise
- **Suncrest products:** StringMax 250 kW inverters, VaultBESS, SunSight
- **EPC partner:** A Gold-tier Suncrest certified partner
- **Results in year one:** 5.6 GWh of solar generation, 31% lower power bill, 4,100 tonnes of CO₂ avoided

## The challenge

The plant runs three shifts, seven days a week, and consumes about 24 GWh of electricity a year. Power was its second-largest cost after raw cotton, and a sharp rise in demand charges had squeezed margins. At the same time, European fashion brands buying from the plant began asking for verified Scope 2 emissions data and a credible decarbonisation roadmap as a condition of future orders.

The sustainability team had two constraints. First, the roofs were old asbestos-cement sheets over some sheds, limiting where modules could go. Second, peak demand came in the evening shift, when solar output is lowest — so simply adding panels would not reduce demand charges.

## The solution

Suncrest's applications engineers worked with the EPC partner to design a system around the plant's load profile rather than its roof area alone.

- **Rooftop solar:** 4 MWp of modules on the four structurally sound sheds and two new metal-roof warehouses, using 16 StringMax 250 kW inverters. String inverters allowed each roof section to be optimised independently despite different orientations.
- **Battery storage:** A 1 MW / 2 MWh VaultBESS container charges from surplus midday solar and discharges during the 6 pm to 8 pm peak, holding the plant's contract demand below its sanctioned limit.
- **SunSight Optimise:** The platform forecasts next-day solar output and production schedules, then sets the battery dispatch plan automatically. It also produces a monthly carbon report aligned to the customer's buyer audits.

Installation was completed in 14 weeks with no production shutdown; each shed was connected during scheduled weekly maintenance windows.

## Results

| Metric | Before | After year one |
|---|---|---|
| Annual grid consumption | 24.0 GWh | 18.4 GWh |
| Peak contract demand | 5.8 MVA | 4.9 MVA |
| Annual electricity cost | INR 19.2 crore | INR 13.2 crore |
| Renewable share | 0% | 23% |

Simple payback is estimated at 4.2 years, including the battery. Plant availability for the solar system was 99.4%, above the 99% guarantee in the Suncrest Assured O&M contract.

Voltage dips that had previously tripped air-jet looms two or three times a month also dropped sharply, because the VaultBESS power-conversion system provides fast reactive-power support.

Encouraged by the results, the customer has committed to a larger programme: captive solar at its Surat unit, additional storage to cover the evening shift fully, and a group captive power purchase agreement to push its renewable share above 45% by 2027 on the way to net-zero operations.
