# Case Study: 3x Order Throughput for a D2C Brand

*How a fast-growing direct-to-consumer beauty brand scaled from 12,000 to 38,000 orders a day without adding floor space.*

## The customer

The customer is a Mumbai-based D2C beauty and personal-care brand that sells through its own website and app, leading marketplaces and quick-commerce platforms. After raising a growth round, the brand set an ambitious goal: next-day delivery in the top 25 Indian cities and readiness for a festive season in which order volumes were forecast to triple.

## The challenge

The brand operated a single 85,000 sq ft fulfilment centre near Bhiwandi with manual person-to-goods picking. Pickers walked up to 14 km per shift. On normal days the site shipped about 12,000 orders; during the previous festive season it had hired 400 temporary workers, yet still built a backlog of more than 60,000 orders and saw picking errors rise to 1.8 percent.

The existing warehouse software could not direct automation, did not support batch and expiry tracking, and required manual file uploads for two marketplaces. Leasing a second warehouse would have taken nine months and added fixed cost the team did not want to carry.

## The solution

Cargolane ran a three-week design sprint that modelled SKU velocity, order profiles and seasonal peaks. The agreed design combined:

- **LaneFlow WMS**, replacing the legacy system, with native connectors to the brand's storefront, four marketplaces and two courier aggregators, and batch and expiry control for cosmetics.
- **110 LaneBot AMRs** feeding 14 goods-to-person pick stations, supplied on **Robotics-as-a-Service** so the brand paid a monthly fee instead of investing capital.
- **A LaneSort pouch sorter** for order consolidation and courier-wise sorting at dispatch.
- **30 additional flex robots** for the October–November peak season.

The project went live in 13 weeks. The site kept shipping throughout: Cargolane converted one zone at a time over four weekends.

## The results

Within four months of go-live:

- **3x throughput per picker**, from 95 to 290 order lines per hour.
- **Daily capacity rose from 12,000 to 38,000 orders** in the same 85,000 sq ft building, thanks to denser pod storage.
- **Picking accuracy improved to 99.93 percent**, and returns due to wrong items fell by 70 percent.
- **Festive season peak of 41,000 orders a day** handled with 120 temporary workers instead of 400, and no backlog beyond 24 hours.
- **Cost per order fell 34 percent**, with payback on the RaaS subscription against the previous labour model in about 16 months.
- **New associates became productive in one shift**, compared with two weeks under the manual process.

## In the customer's words

"We expected robots to help with speed. What surprised us was how calm the festive season felt. Our team watched the dashboards instead of fighting fires," said the brand's Head of Warehousing.

"Robotics-as-a-Service meant we could put our funding into product and marketing rather than steel and batteries," added the Chief Operating Officer.

## What's next

The brand is now opening a second fulfilment centre in Gurugram to serve north India, which will run on the same LaneFlow tenant with a smaller AMR fleet from day one. It is also piloting LaneRoute TMS to plan shipments to quick-commerce warehouses and modern-trade distribution centres.
